title: "Tapas Culture in Madrid"
---

I'm in Madrid this week, and I've decided the city's real operating system is tapas.

Dinner here isn't an event you sit down for at 7pm — it's a slow drift from bar to bar. A caña of beer comes with a little plate you didn't order: olives, a sliver of tortilla, maybe some jamón if the bartender likes you. You stand, you eat, you talk, you move on. Repeat until somehow it's midnight.

What I love most is how social the whole thing is. Nobody is rushing you out for the next reservation, because there is no reservation. The table — or more often the bar counter — is just where the conversation happens to be standing.

A few things I've learned so far:

- **Order the house specialty.** Every bar does one thing better than anyone else, and the locals will tell you what it is if you ask.
- **Tortilla opinions are serious.** Runny center or fully set is a genuine debate. (Runny. Obviously.)
- **Pace yourself.** It's a marathon eaten in bites.
